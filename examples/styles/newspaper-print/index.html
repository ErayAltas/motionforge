Night trams are back
Service resumes after a year of track work
The repaired line near Pier Square. Illustration for this preview.

The night service across the old town will run again from Friday, the city transport office said, ending a year in which late workers and students relied on two crowded bus routes.

Crews replaced the worn rails on the hill section in the spring and tested the new overhead wire through the summer. The final inspection was signed on Monday.

Traders on the harbour front welcomed the news.

Trams will leave the depot every twenty minutes between midnight and five, with an extra car on Saturdays. Fares stay the same as the day service.

Officials had promised a return before the winter. “We expect the first tram at 23:40 on Friday,” a spokesperson said, adding that the timetable would be printed at every stop by Thursday.

Residents of the upper streets asked for quieter wheels on the curve by the school. The office said new dampers were fitted in August and that noise readings would be published next month.

A ferry link at the north pier will wait for the last tram on weekends.

Night travel in the city has grown since the harbour bars were allowed to open later, according to the office's own counts. The bus routes that replaced the trams carried twice their planned load on some weekends.

Drivers for the new service were hired in the summer and trained on the hill section after dark. Two extra cars were borrowed from the depot in the east.

The office will review the timetable in the spring and has asked riders to send comments through the stop boards and the city website.

Repairs to the bridge spur are planned for next year.
